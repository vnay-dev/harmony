# Harmony Tutor Script

This is the script Harmony should speak.

`documentation/tutor-dialogue-inventory.md` records what the current implementation says. This file replaces that wording for future voice recording. It does not change the app.

Spoken lines are written as the listener should hear them. Sharp notes are spoken as "C sharp", not "C#". The on-screen label can remain `C#`.

Lines marked **Not yet wired** belong to the assisted lesson, but the app cannot yet tell that assisted practice succeeded. Record them. Do not play the success handoff until that verification exists.

---

## Voice principles

Harmony sounds like a patient vocal teacher who also has the pace of a meditation guide.

- Warm, quiet, and unhurried.
- Short sentences, with room between them.
- Encouraging without cheerleading.
- Humble. Harmony offers a sound and a way through. It does not grade the singer.
- When something does not land, the next line teaches a smaller step. It does not repeat the same command.
- The singer never needs to know what the app is measuring.

Do not say: pitch, frequency, cents, Hz, incorrect, failed, unable to detect, retry, submit, confirm, done.

Do not say "Go."

Do not say the singer is ready, or that the Shruti has been found, until a solo listen has actually succeeded. Singing along with the reference is practice. It is not the result.

Struggle deepens the help:

1. First miss on the starting note: reassure, and offer an easier way to sing.
2. Next miss: Harmony lets them hear a sound, then asks them to try it alone.
3. A later miss: slow down, and listen to that sound again.
4. On a range note, the first miss listens to the same sound again.
5. The second miss on a range note becomes practice together, with Harmony still audible.
6. Only after that practice has truly succeeded does Harmony ask for the sound alone.

"That's okay" is for a miss. "That's lovely" is for an answer Harmony can use. "Beautiful" is only for the moment they can stop singing. They are not interchangeable.

---

## 1. Welcome

Spoken once, in this order, before any listening.

1. Hi. I'll help you find a comfortable Shruti.
2. You don't need to know anything about singing.
3. Settle in, and follow my voice.
4. Let's begin gently.

---

## 2. Starting note

5. Sing or hum a sound that feels comfortable.
6. Stay with it for a few seconds.

Then the solo countdown. The same three lines are used every time the singer will sing alone, including this first turn. There is no separate "get ready", and there is no "Go."

7. Let's try singing it again in 3...
8. 2...
9. 1...

Harmony is silent. The singer holds the sound.

If a sound is heard:

10. Beautiful. You can stop there.
11. Now let's find a comfortable place for your voice.

Then the lower note.

If no sound is heard, go to Starting note retry. Do not say "Beautiful."

---

## 3. Starting note retry

First time the starting note is not heard:

12. That's okay. Let's try once more.
13. A soft, steady hum is enough.

Then 7, 8, 9. Harmony stays silent. The singer tries again.

Do not announce that a countdown is coming. The countdown is the cue to begin.

---

## 4. Guided demonstration

Second time the starting note is not heard. This is the first time Harmony offers a sound.

14. That's all right. I'll let you hear a sound first.
15. Just listen.

Play the sound. Harmony does not talk over it.

When the sound has finished:

16. Now try that sound.

Then 7, 8, 9. The singer tries alone. Harmony is not playing.

If that solo try is heard, say 10, then 11, and continue.

If it is still not heard:

17. We'll take this more gently.
18. Let's listen once more.

Play the sound again. Then 16, then 7, 8, 9.

Repeat 17 and 18 if later starting-note tries are still not heard. Do not switch this step into sing-along. Harmony does not yet have the singer's own note, so singing a fixed sound together would teach the wrong thing.

"Just listen." means the sound is new and they should not sing yet.

"Let's listen once more." means they have already heard it.

"Now try that sound." means the sound has stopped and the next countdown is theirs alone.

---

## 5. Lower note

First time, for the first Shruti:

19. Let's try a slightly lower sound.
15. Just listen.

Play the lower sound. Then:

16. Now try that sound.
7. Let's try singing it again in 3...
8. 2...
9. 1...

If it is heard:

10. Beautiful. You can stop there.

Then ask whether they could hear it clearly. See section 8.

If it is not heard, do not repeat the whole introduction. See Failure / recovery: first miss on a note they have already heard.

Later, when moving to another Shruti, use section 7's moving lines instead of "Let's try a slightly lower sound." The listen, try, and countdown stay the same.

---

## 6. Middle / Pa

20. Let's try one in the middle.
15. Just listen.

Play the middle sound. Then 16, then 7, 8, 9.

If it is heard: 10, then the upper note. There is no question in the middle.

If it is not heard: the same first-miss and assisted path as any other range note.

Do not add "Nice" or another compliment here. "Beautiful. You can stop there." has already closed the previous note.

---

## 7. Upper note

Within the same Shruti:

21. Now a slightly higher sound.
15. Just listen.

Play the higher sound. Then 16, then 7, 8, 9.

If it is heard: 10, then "How did that feel?"

Moving to the next Shruti upward:

22. Let's move a little higher.
15. Just listen.

Then the lower sound of that new Shruti, then 16, then the solo countdown.

Moving to the next Shruti downward:

23. Let's move a little lower.
15. Just listen.

Then the lower sound of that new Shruti, then 16, then the solo countdown.

"Now a slightly higher sound." stays inside the Shruti they are already trying.

"Let's move a little higher." and "Let's move a little lower." mean a new Shruti.

---

## 8. Comfortable / not comfortable questions

After the lower sound was heard:

24. Could you hear that sound clearly?

They may say yes or no, or use the buttons.

- Yes: 25. That's lovely. Then the middle note.
- No: 26. That's okay. Let's try a little higher. Then move upward.
- Unclear words: 27. Sorry, I didn't quite catch that. 28. Yes or no is enough.
- Silence: 29. There's no hurry.

After the upper sound was heard:

30. How did that feel?

- Comfortable: 25. That's lovely. If this is the comfortable place, go to Completion. If the lesson is still exploring upward, move a little higher.
- Not comfortable: 31. That's all right. We'll keep this comfortable. Then finish on the last comfortable Shruti, ease lower, or close gently if there is nowhere comfortable left. Do not describe which of those the lesson chose.
- Unclear words: 27. Sorry, I didn't quite catch that. 32. You can say comfortable, or not comfortable.
- Silence: 29. There's no hurry.

Ask once more after the first unclear answer. If it is still unclear, stop asking aloud and leave the buttons.

Do not say "Good." It sounds like a score.

---

## 9. Assisted practice

This is the lesson after the singer has already tried a range note alone twice. It should feel like practice with a teacher, not like the same test starting over.

**Not yet wired:** the app cannot yet tell the singer's voice from the reference. Until it can, it must not pretend this practice succeeded, and it must not continue as if the note was learned.

When practice together begins:

33. That's okay. Let's practice it together so you can get familiar with the sound.
34. Listen carefully, and sing along with me.

Then the assisted countdown. The reference must stay silent until these lines and the countdown have finished. After "1...", the reference stays on while they sing.

35. Let's sing together in 3...
8. 2...
9. 1...

"Listen carefully, and sing along with me." is allowed only here, because Harmony is about to remain audible.

Do not say "Beautiful. You can stop there." at the end of this practice. The reference was playing. That is not a solo result.

If this pass does not yet count as learned:

36. That's okay. Let's practice that sound together once more.

Then 34, 35, 8, and 9 again.

**Not yet wired:** choosing 36 instead of the solo return. Today the app always behaves as if the pass was not confirmed.

If a second practice still does not lead to a solo success, stop pushing that note. Use the closing lines in Failure / recovery.

---

## 10. Return to solo singing

**Not yet wired.** Say this only after assisted practice has actually succeeded. Do not say it after the timer ends, and do not say it merely because the reference finished.

37. Now try that sound on your own.

Then the solo countdown, with Harmony silent:

7. Let's try singing it again in 3...
8. 2...
9. 1...

The singer sings alone.

- If that solo singing is heard: 10. Beautiful. You can stop there. Then continue the normal path for that note: the question, the next note, or Completion. The Shruti is still not announced unless this was the final successful step of the whole lesson.
- If that solo singing is not heard: 36. That's okay. Let's practice that sound together once more. Return to assisted practice.

Do not say "Beautiful. You're ready." Practice is not the result. The solo listen is.

---

## 11. Completion

Say the celebration, pause, then the name. One name per lesson.

38. Wonderful. We found a comfortable Shruti for you.

Then one of:

39. Your Shruti is C.
40. Your Shruti is C sharp.
41. Your Shruti is D.
42. Your Shruti is D sharp.
43. Your Shruti is E.
44. Your Shruti is F.
45. Your Shruti is F sharp.
46. Your Shruti is G.
47. Your Shruti is G sharp.
48. Your Shruti is A.
49. Your Shruti is A sharp.
50. Your Shruti is B.

Template: `Your Shruti is {spoken name}.`

Spoken names: C, C sharp, D, D sharp, E, F, F sharp, G, G sharp, A, A sharp, B.

This is the first time Harmony names the Shruti.

## 12. Failure / recovery

Use the smallest next step. Do not cycle the same sentence for every kind of miss.

| Moment | Say |
|--------|-----|
| Starting note, first miss | That's okay. Let's try once more. / A soft, steady hum is enough. |
| Starting note, second miss | That's all right. I'll let you hear a sound first. / Just listen. |
| Starting note, later miss | We'll take this more gently. / Let's listen once more. |
| Range note, first miss | That's okay. Let's listen once more. |
| Range note, second miss | Assisted practice, section 9 |
| After a reference, before they sing alone | Now try that sound. |
| Lower sound was not clear enough | That's okay. Let's try a little higher. |
| Upper sound was not comfortable | That's all right. We'll keep this comfortable. |
| Assisted pass needs another try | That's okay. Let's practice that sound together once more. |
| Assisted success, back to singing alone | Now try that sound on your own. |
| Spoken answer was unclear | Sorry, I didn't quite catch that. Then the short yes/no or comfort cue. |
| No answer yet | There's no hurry. |
| Nowhere comfortable left, or practice could not continue | We haven't found a comfortable Shruti just yet. / We can try again whenever you like. |

Closing, in order:

51. We haven't found a comfortable Shruti just yet.
52. We can try again whenever you like.

Stop does not speak. The singer asked for quiet.

"That's okay. Let's try once more." is only the first starting-note miss.

"That's okay. Let's listen once more." is the first miss after they have already heard that range note.

"That's okay. Let's practice that sound together once more." is only another pass of singing together.

Also absent from the voice script, on purpose:

- "Go." and "Get ready."
- "I'll let you know when to begin."
- "Session stopped." and "You can try again whenever you're ready." Stop stays silent.
- The unused constant "That's okay. Listen once more."
- Controller messages that are not spoken today, including "Let's try that again in a moment." and "That's okay — your Shruti is ready."

New lines that were not a rewrite of an existing sentence:

- S13 A soft, steady hum is enough.
- S19 That's okay. Let's listen once more.
- S33 There's no hurry.

S13 replaces a repeated instruction, and S19 replaces using the starting-note retry on a range note. They are listed here because the wording is new, not a light edit.
